**Title: Carbon Border Pricing and Trade Diversion: A Difference-in-Differences Analysis of the EU CBAM Sectors**

**Supervisor:** Marten Ovaere 

**Context**
Since 1 January 2026, the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) has been in force in its definitive regime: importers of steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen pay a carbon price at the border on the embedded emissions of their imports. The goal is to counter carbon leakage — the relocation of carbon-intensive production to countries with less stringent climate policy. Whether CBAM actually achieves this, and whether it instead leads to circumvention (resource reshuffling, shifting countries of origin, light processing into just-out-of-scope products), has barely been established empirically. The transitional phase (Oct 2023 – Dec 2025) and the first year of the definitive regime now provide, for the first time, the data to measure anticipatory and actual trade responses.

**RQ** 
Do import volumes, countries of origin and product mix within the CBAM sectors shift in response to the (announced and effective) CBAM price, and are there signs of circumvention? How does the response vary with the carbon intensity of the country of origin and with the presence of a domestic carbon price?

**Data** 
Eurostat Comext, the open EU trade database with bilateral import flows at the level of the Combined Nomenclature (CN, the eight-digit EU goods nomenclature that further subdivides the international HS codes), from which the six CBAM sectors can be precisely delineated. [https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/international-trade-in-goods/database](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/international-trade-in-goods/database)  

Supplemented with the aggregate registry statistics from the Commission review report COM(2025) 783 (emissions by sector, country of origin and reporting method), the published default values and benchmarks, and the CBAM certificate price series. Country-of-origin carbon intensities via national grid and sectoral emission factors.

**Approach** 
You build a panel dataset and use a difference-in-differences design: CBAM goods as treatment, comparable non-CBAM goods or non-EU destinations as control, around the announcement and implementation dates. You test for shifts in volume and origin, and for circumvention signals (e.g. growth of just-out-of-scope CN codes or transit through third countries). You then split the response by carbon intensity of the country of origin and by existing carbon pricing, to see whether the trade response is consistent with the carbon-leakage mechanism CBAM is designed to address.

**What this gives you** 
- An empirically grounded estimate of the early trade effect of the world 's first carbon border levy, and of where circumvention risks concentrate.  
- Insight into how climate policy and international trade interact, experience with causal identification on large trade datasets, and the skill to draw policy-relevant conclusions from raw data.

**Requirements** 
Strong affinity for data analysis (Python or R) and interest in climate policy and international trade.
